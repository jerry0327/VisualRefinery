# Domain Profiles

Use only the profile(s) relevant to the task. These are overlays on the core VisualRefinery workflow, not separate skills.

## Academic & research communication

Prioritize:

- accurate representation of methods and results;
- distinction between primary findings, secondary analyses, and interpretation;
- uncertainty, confidence intervals, limitations, and study design;
- current and appropriate citations;
- figures that preserve denominators, units, and uncertainty;
- reproducible description of analytic choices where relevant.

Avoid overstating causality, generalizability, or precision.

For conference materials, surface the research question and primary result early while keeping methodological detail available in notes, captions, appendices, or supporting pages.

## Business & strategy

Prioritize:

- the decision to be made;
- current state and baseline;
- key drivers;
- trade-offs;
- financial or operational magnitude;
- scenarios;
- risks and dependencies;
- explicit recommendations and next actions.

Use executive summaries that answer the question, not merely describe the document.

Do not create scorecards with arbitrary weights or false precision unless the scoring method is defined and defensible.

## Education & training

Prioritize:

- a clear learning objective;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- cognitive load;
- mechanism and causal understanding;
- worked examples;
- common errors or misconceptions;
- retrieval cues and takeaways.

Use visual progression to reveal complex processes in stages when useful.

Do not add content merely to make the material look comprehensive if it dilutes the learning goal.

## Technical & engineering communication

Prioritize:

- system boundaries;
- interfaces;
- assumptions;
- data and control flow;
- failure modes;
- operational constraints;
- performance or reliability characteristics;
- trade-offs and alternatives;
- implementation consequences.

Use diagrams when relationships are more important than prose. Keep diagrams layered so a reader can understand the system before reading implementation details.

## Data & analytics

Prioritize:

- metric definitions;
- denominators and time windows;
- comparison groups;
- uncertainty and missingness;
- target or benchmark context;
- validated drivers rather than speculative stories;
- clear separation between descriptive and causal claims.

Charts should answer questions. Avoid displaying metrics merely because they are available.

## Policy & public information

Prioritize:

- plain language;
- legal or policy currency;
- distinction between mandatory rules, recommendations, and interpretation;
- practical consequences;
- accessibility;
- multilingual or low-literacy considerations when relevant;
- dates and jurisdiction.

Avoid hiding important conditions in footnotes.

## Medical & clinical communication

Use this profile for professional medical, clinical, scientific, or health-service artifacts.

Prioritize:

- current guidelines and primary evidence where appropriate;
- exact doses, criteria, cutoffs, timing, contraindications, and exceptions when they drive decisions;
- clinician cognitive load and time pressure;
- action sequence and decision thresholds;
- uncertainty and consequences of delay;
- de-identification of patient information;
- separation of evidence, local practice, and expert judgment.

For teaching decks, use one dominant clinical message per slide and decision-oriented visuals rather than encyclopedic lists.

High-stakes patient-specific decisions require appropriate qualified review. Do not make the visual polish imply a level of clinical certainty the evidence does not support.

## Travel & hospitality

Prioritize the real experience, not only promotional descriptions.

Where relevant, consider:

- walking distance and surface;
- stairs and accessibility;
- transfer frequency;
- arrival and departure timing;
- realistic dwell time;
- meal timing;
- room assignment variability;
- hotel age and layout;
- bath, pool, spa, breakfast, and service characteristics;
- ticket prices and included versus optional costs;
- season, weather, crowding, and closure risk;
- the downside of a poor assignment or substitution.

Use recurring review patterns as contextual evidence, not as substitutes for official facts.

Do not invent hotel assignments, restaurant names, room types, inclusions, or prices that are not supported.

## Product & service comparison

Prioritize:

- the user's actual decision criteria;
- total cost, not only headline price;
- key specifications;
- reliability and support;
- compatibility;
- recurring review patterns;
- constraints and switching costs;
- best-fit scenarios rather than one universal winner.

Separate manufacturer claims from independent evaluation.

## Marketing & communications

Prioritize:

- the intended audience and action;
- message hierarchy;
- defensible proof points;
- brand or campaign constraints;
- consistency across touchpoints;
- clear calls to action;
- accessibility and platform context.

Avoid unsupported superlatives and generic hype. Strong communication is specific about why a claim matters.

## Legal / regulatory communication

Prioritize:

- jurisdiction;
- effective date;
- authoritative text;
- definitions;
- mandatory versus optional provisions;
- exceptions and thresholds;
- procedural sequence;
- clear separation between source text, analysis, and recommendation.

Do not allow simplified visual summaries to erase conditions that materially change legal interpretation. Qualified legal review may be required for consequential use.

## Financial communication

Prioritize:

- units, currency, period, and accounting basis;
- assumptions;
- sensitivity and scenarios;
- distinction between historical, forecast, and illustrative figures;
- risk and uncertainty;
- comparability across periods or entities.

Do not use chart scaling or formatting that exaggerates small differences. Do not present forecasts as facts.

## Creative / editorial communication

Prioritize:

- a coherent point of view;
- rhythm and pacing;
- image-text relationships;
- strong captions;
- intentional typography;
- narrative continuity;
- source and rights awareness.

A more expressive design is acceptable when it supports the editorial intent, but readability and content integrity still apply.

## Creating a new profile

Add a new domain profile only when the domain has constraints that are not already covered by the core skill.

A useful profile should be compact and answer:

1. What does this domain make unusually important?
2. What errors are especially costly?
3. What information must remain explicit?
4. What visual forms are particularly useful or dangerous?

Do not duplicate the entire core workflow inside a profile.
