# QA Checklist

Use this checklist before declaring a substantial visual artifact finished.

Not every item applies to every artifact. Apply the relevant checks and record exceptions when useful.

## A. Content integrity

- [ ] The artifact answers the actual user or reader question.
- [ ] Load-bearing claims have adequate support.
- [ ] Time-sensitive facts were checked against current sources.
- [ ] Dates, units, denominators, currencies, and comparison conditions are explicit.
- [ ] Source-derived meaning was not silently altered during editing.
- [ ] Facts, interpretation, inference, and recommendation are not misleadingly conflated.
- [ ] Missing information is marked as missing rather than invented.
- [ ] Conflicting reliable sources are represented appropriately.
- [ ] Quotations and citations accurately reflect their sources.

## B. Editorial quality

- [ ] The opening makes the purpose clear.
- [ ] Headings communicate findings or useful orientation.
- [ ] Sections follow a coherent sequence.
- [ ] Repetition has been removed.
- [ ] Paragraphs synthesize evidence rather than stack source snippets.
- [ ] Generic AI-style filler and repetitive rhetorical formulas have been removed.
- [ ] Caveats are placed where they affect interpretation.
- [ ] The conclusion follows from the evidence.
- [ ] The reader can identify the next action or key takeaway when the artifact requires one.

## C. Information architecture

- [ ] Every page or slide has a dominant job.
- [ ] Page sequence supports the reader's task.
- [ ] Dense content has been structured rather than merely compressed.
- [ ] Sparse pages are intentionally sparse or have been rebalanced.
- [ ] Tables, charts, diagrams, and prose are used for the type of information each handles best.
- [ ] Secondary detail is moved to notes, appendices, or supporting sections when appropriate.

## D. Typography and layout

- [ ] Title, heading, body, caption, and citation levels are visually distinct.
- [ ] Body text is readable at real viewing size.
- [ ] Line spacing and paragraph spacing are comfortable.
- [ ] Margins and alignment are consistent.
- [ ] Related elements are grouped clearly.
- [ ] No text box or cell is overfilled.
- [ ] No object extends unintentionally beyond the canvas.
- [ ] Repeated page types use consistent geometry.
- [ ] Whitespace creates hierarchy rather than accidental emptiness.

## E. Images and visual assets

- [ ] Every major image or illustration has a reason to be present.
- [ ] Images are relevant to the subject.
- [ ] No image is visibly stretched or distorted.
- [ ] Effective resolution is adequate.
- [ ] No watermarks or unauthorized-looking assets are present.
- [ ] Screenshots do not expose irrelevant private or sensitive information.
- [ ] Image crops preserve the important subject.
- [ ] Captions add information rather than repeat the obvious.
- [ ] Asset provenance is recorded where required.

## F. Charts, tables, and diagrams

- [ ] The visual form matches the analytical question.
- [ ] Units and denominators are visible.
- [ ] Important baselines and comparison points are not misleadingly omitted.
- [ ] Chart titles state the takeaway or question clearly.
- [ ] Labels and legends are readable.
- [ ] Uncertainty is shown when relevant.
- [ ] Color is not the only carrier of essential meaning.
- [ ] Table text is readable without excessive zoom.
- [ ] Diagram direction and branch logic are unambiguous.
- [ ] Decorative complexity has not overwhelmed the message.

## G. Contrast and accessibility

- [ ] Text has sufficient contrast against its background.
- [ ] Light text is not placed directly over bright or busy image areas.
- [ ] Information is not encoded by red/green or color alone.
- [ ] Reading order is logical.
- [ ] Essential visuals have descriptions or alt text where the format supports it.
- [ ] Critical information is not hidden only in notes or hover states.
- [ ] Language and jargon density fit the intended audience.

## H. Full-page manual inspection

- [ ] Every page or slide has been rendered.
- [ ] A contact sheet / montage has been reviewed for rhythm and consistency.
- [ ] Every complete page / slide has been inspected at readable scale.
- [ ] Dense or complex pages have received additional enlarged inspection where useful.
- [ ] No inspection relied only on crops.
- [ ] All corrected pages have been re-rendered.
- [ ] The complete artifact has been rechecked after corrections for regressions.

Look specifically for:

- clipped titles;
- truncated body text;
- hidden table rows;
- overlapping text and graphics;
- off-canvas elements;
- orphaned icons or shapes;
- bad line wrapping;
- low-contrast labels;
- image crops that remove the subject;
- inconsistent alignment;
- accidental one-line spillovers;
- citations too small to read;
- slides or pages that feel visually unfinished.

## I. Technical validation

Where applicable:

- [ ] The file opens in an independent viewer.
- [ ] Text extraction contains no missing or corrupted glyphs.
- [ ] Fonts are embedded, portable, or safely substitutable.
- [ ] Links work.
- [ ] Bookmarks / navigation work.
- [ ] Page and slide numbering are correct.
- [ ] Images retain adequate resolution after export.
- [ ] Editable objects remain editable where promised.
- [ ] PDF / image exports match the editable source sufficiently.
- [ ] No unexpected warnings or repair prompts appear on open.

## J. High-stakes domain checks

When relevant:

- [ ] Clinical doses, thresholds, contraindications, and guideline versions were rechecked.
- [ ] Legal or regulatory jurisdiction and effective dates are explicit.
- [ ] Financial periods, currencies, assumptions, and forecast status are explicit.
- [ ] Safety-critical technical values and interfaces were verified.
- [ ] Personally identifying or confidential information has been handled appropriately.
- [ ] The artifact does not visually overstate certainty.

## K. Delivery and recoverability

- [ ] Final files use stable descriptive names.
- [ ] Editable sources are included when required.
- [ ] The known-good prior version was preserved until the revision passed QA.
- [ ] Asset sources and licenses are recorded when required.
- [ ] A claim ledger or source record is retained for substantial evidence-driven work.
- [ ] A QA log is retained when auditability matters.
- [ ] The delivered artifact contains no internal prompt, developer, revision, or chain-of-thought language.

## L. Honest completion

- [ ] Every claimed verification step was actually performed.
- [ ] Any check that could not be performed is identified as unverified.
- [ ] The final delivery distinguishes completed work from remaining limitations.

If a material defect remains, the artifact is not finished.
